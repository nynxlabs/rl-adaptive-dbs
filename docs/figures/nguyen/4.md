## Gates (exit criteria)

Gate code: `scripts/digitization/nguyen_gates.py` (`fig4_gates`, `fig4_abort_check`, tables `FIG4_CHECKS`, `FIG4_REQUIRED_KEYS`); panel wiring in `scripts/figures/papers/nguyen/4/plot.py`; tracker rows in `scripts/figures/papers/promote.py` (`NGUYEN_GATE_ROWS["4"]`). Gates recalibrated **Oct 17 2026**.

**Superseded:** the 2026-10-03 rebuild (retired key names, ±25 % tolerances, absolute reward thresholds) is replaced by the calibrated keys below. Its reasons still hold:

- a trailing smoothing window lagged our curve about 10 episodes behind the paper's non-causal curve;
- Eq. (7) leaves δ, τ and the normalization of $d$ unstated, so reward gates are scale-free.

**Tolerances.** A faithful run is the digitized paper curve plus one seed's deviation from its config's mean curve, with the deviation inflated by $\sqrt{N/(N-1)}$. Each tolerance is the smallest clean number (two significant figures; milestones in steps of 5 episodes) at which at least 90 % of those synthetic runs pass the gate key. A key with two checks widens both together. The pool is 58 runs: wu1500 ×13, s5d1 ×13, and audit1 base, net1, net1seq and net1seq100 ×8 each. The calibration is `scripts/probes/nguyen_gate_calibration.py`. Pearson checks compare with the paper, where r = 1, so the tolerance is $1 - r_{\min}$.

**How it scores:** our per-episode series is smoothed with a **centred 20-episode moving average**. Paper anchors come from the digitized `Smoothed` series interpolated onto a whole-episode grid (0–499). **Progress** runs from 0 at the ep 0–50 smoothed level to 1 at the ep 150–500 smoothed level. $t_{10}$, $t_{50}$ and $t_{90}$ are the first episodes reaching 10 / 50 / 90 % progress. The digitized `Raw` series is a traced outline of the noisy band (out along one edge, back along the other). Code splits it into a lower and an upper edge (`raw_outline_edges`) for the overlay and the logged band stats; it is never sorted into one curve.

**Report-only (logged, never in `pass`):**

**Early abort** (`--abort-on-fail`, candidates): a doomed-run check only, decidable from episode **150**. It aborts when length or reward **progress over ep 120–150** is below **0.5**. ## Lineage / diagnosis (2026-07-31)

### What we know from Fig 3

Untreated **PD On** α–β (7–35 Hz, 100 ms): mean ~291, Q1 ~262. θ=150 sits **below** typical PD On mass (~0.6% of untreated windows < 150). Sustained sub-threshold control needs **DBS suppression**, not luck.

### v2 (main, Jul 27) — wrong reward sign

Distance term used **`+d`** when above θ → rewards in **positive** millions. Gates failed: late reward worse than early; lengths stuck at 25.

### v4 (paper-faithful) — sign fixed, still no early-stop

Eq. (7) distance **`−d`**; rewards ~−10⁶ → −6×10⁵. Passes scale + late>early; **fails** length drop and near-zero late reward.

| Evidence | Finding |
|----------|---------|
| Only **2 / 500** episodes length < 25 (eps 3, 31) | Early-stop almost never fires |
| Late energy ~141/ep vs early ~497/ep | Policy learns **weaker** stimulation |
| Paper end triple ~262 nA / 78.65 Hz / 1 ms | Higher energy than late v4 policy |
| Per-step $d ≈ (αβ−150)^2$ ~10⁴ when α–β~260 | Dominates τ=1 / δE=0.01·E |

**Hypothesis:** agent can shave −δE without ever reaching θ; terminal bonus (≤25·τ) is tiny vs $d$, so length never drops.

### Open question (resolved)

Can **any** fixed STN triple on Kumaravelu + `alpha_beta_power` (7–35 Hz, 100 ms) achieve **3 consecutive** steps < 150?

**Result (2026-07-31, seeds 0–4):** θ **is reachable** — not a structural plant blocker. (One-off reachability probe; findings kept here.)

| Triple | mean α–β | streak≥3 |
|--------|----------|----------|
| init 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 | 269 | 0/5 |
| paper 78.65 Hz / 1 ms / 262 | 147 | 3/5 (early-stop ep 3) |
| 130 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 | 118 | 3/5 |
| 500 / 130 / 1.0 | 269 | 0/5 (same as init — wide pulse?) |
| min energy 10 Hz / 50 | 262 | 0/5 |

**Conclusion:** agent must learn **frequency increase** toward ~80–130 Hz, not energy collapse. v4 failed because raw $d≈10^4$ makes above-θ returns nearly flat in α–β; only $-δE$ gradient remains → low-energy, high-α–β policy.

## Paper-faithful update rule (Oct 3 2026)

v158 departed from the paper in two ways:

- **Replay cadence 32.** The paper fixes it: weights update "after collecting every 128 state transitions". Earlier entries (v58 onward) call the cadence paper-silent; that was wrong.
- **A target network that never synced.** The paper's Q-target bootstraps from the online weights $\theta$, with no separate target network. v158 kept a hard-copy target every 200 updates but made only 177, so for the whole run it bootstrapped from the random initial network.

`fig4_nguyen_config` now uses cadence **128** and no target network (`target_update_period=0`). Each flush runs **4** minibatches of 32 (`replay_update_steps`; paper-silent convention — 4 × 32 covers the 128 new transitions and keeps roughly v158's SGD budget). The other v158 knobs were tuned under the old rule and may need re-tuning; v158 stays shipped until a run under the new rule passes the gates.

## Authors' published DSQN implementation (Oct 4 2026)

**Evidence that the literal rule doesn't train.** About 30 runs under the literal rule above (no target network, cadence 128, Eq. 7 reward, paper bounds and init) failed the gates. The best (S2 step sizes, 8 minibatches per flush) was run on three seeds to episode 500:

| Seed | Mean length, ep 101–150 | Mean length, ep 451–500 | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 16.1 | 14.0 | Plateaus around 14–17 |
| 1 | 18.9 | 22.4 | Regresses |
| 2 | 23.6 | 25.0 | Collapses to 100% timeouts after ep ~300 |

All three runs fail most required gates. The drift-and-collapse pattern is the usual instability of bootstrapping from constantly changing online weights.

**The authors' own implementation.** A June 2026 follow-up from the same group (Nguyen, Josephson, Teodorescu, Cauwenberghs, Eshraghian, *Neuromorphic Energy-Aware Learning for Adaptive Deep Brain Stimulation*, [arXiv 2606.28600](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28600v1)) trains the same DSQN and publishes its full training loop (Algorithm S1) and hyperparameters (Table S3). It uses:

- a target SNN θ′ with soft updates, τ_tgt = 0.005, after every gradient step; the TD target is max Q_θ′;
- the SmoothL1 loss and the AdamW optimizer;
- `clip_grad_value_(100)`, not norm clipping;
- γ 0.99, lr 1e-3, buffer 100k, batch 128, and ε 0.9 → 0.05 over 2,000 steps.

We read the Fig 4 paper's θ in the Q-target as notation shorthand.

**Decision (Oct 4 2026).** Wherever the Fig 4 paper is silent or ambiguous, follow the group's published implementation. Wherever the Fig 4 paper states something, keep it as written: Eq. 7 reward, the 128-transition update cadence, architecture, encoding, θ, init stimulation, and episode count. ## Spike-count tie bug (Oct 4 2026)

**Symptom.** Runs under both update rules learned early, then drifted toward "stimulation off". In `a-s1-k32`, mean amplitude went 171 → 65, frequency 95 → 1 Hz, and α–β 181 → 300 by ep 451–500. Seeds 1–2 of the literal rule degraded the same way.

**Cause.** The control argmax uses output spike counts (§III.B). With 5 unrolled steps, the output units mostly saturate (5 spikes) or stay silent (0), so a head's three counts are usually tied, and `np.argmax` returns index 0 (−1, "decrease"). A probe of the most recent 2,000 replay states in each checkpoint:

| Run | Amplitude head tied | Greedy picks "decrease" (spikes) | Picks "decrease" by membrane Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a-s1-k32` (ep 500) | 100% | 100% | 16% |
| `a-s2-k32` (ep 150) | 98% | 97% | 22% |
| `s2-k8-full`, literal rule (ep 500) | 97% | 92% | 22% |
| `s2-k8-seed2`, literal rule (ep 500) | 94% (all-zero) | 96% | 47% |

The greedy policy lowered amplitude almost every step, whatever the Q-values said. Every earlier Fig 4 run had this bug, including v158. Its narrow bounds and early-episode schedules were probably compensating for it.

**Fix.** Ties now go to the higher output membrane potential, the value the Q-loss trains (`actions._argmax_spikes`). The paper fixes spike-count argmax but leaves tie-breaking unstated, so this is a convention. The authors' follow-up (arXiv 2606.28600, Algorithm S1) selects actions by argmax of accumulated membrane potential outright.

## Plant, observation, and ε findings (Oct 5 2026)

After the tie fix, ~30 runs on the restart plant with GPi-only input traded the start level against the late level: steps small enough for the paper's flat start (~25) plateaued at ~15, and steps that reached the paper's late level (~8) started at ~20. The best run failed 2 gates.

**Early stops are real suppression, not noise.** Probe: 150 random-policy episodes per row, 40 fixed-triple episodes, and 4 episodes per constant action (`t_u = 2`, θ = 150, amplitude step 10).

|---|---|---|

| Random policy, 12.5 Hz / 0.125 ms steps: mean length, early-stop share | 21.4, 29% | 17.6, 52% |
| Random policy, 5 Hz / 0.05 ms | 24.8, 3% | 21.9, 28% |
| Fastest constant action (raise frequency), 5 Hz steps: median stop step | 11.5 | 5.5 |

Random walks reach suppressing frequencies; chance readings below θ almost never come twice in a row. On the restart plant no step size gives both the paper's flat start and a reachable ~8-step stop.

**Continuous plant (`plant_carry`).** `NguyenEnvAdapter` re-integrated every 100 ms step from the network's initial conditions (the same "disconnected restarts" artifact Mehregan Fig 4a had). The follow-up randomizes connectivity and initial conditions *per episode*, so one episode is one continuous simulation. `plant_carry=True` carries plant state across steps, and `envs.plant.dbs.ContinuousPulseTrain` keeps the STN pulse train phase-continuous across segment boundaries (a pulse that crosses a boundary finishes at its own amplitude). With `plant_carry`, 2.5 Hz / 0.025 ms steps give a random-policy start of 24.4 and a fastest stop of 6–7.5 steps.

**All eight CBGT populations (`n_regions=8`).** The observation was GPi only, a v1 choice. The Fig 4 paper encodes spikes per brain region of the CBGT model, and the follow-up observes 8 populations × 10 neurons. The plant now records STN, GPe, and both striatal populations (`record_bg_spikes`), and the adapter stacks cortex exc/inh, striatum D1/D2, STN, GPe, GPi, and thalamus into an 80-neuron × 10-bin matrix. STN fires with the stimulation, so the agent can see what it is applying. This was the largest single gain: late level 8.3–8.9 with no timeouts on the best seeds.

## Frozen DSQN layers, gate calibration, and the passing recipe (Oct 5 2026)

**Frozen layers.** `LIFLayer` spiked with a bare `membrane > threshold`, which has no gradient. Only the output layer's linear weights ever trained; the input and hidden layers stayed at their random init (a direct `backward()` check returned `grad = None` for both). Every Fig 4 run before `surrogate_gradient=atan`, v158 included, trained a fixed random spiking reservoir with a linear readout. The arctan surrogate (snnTorch default, α 2) trains all three layers; the follow-up cites surrogate-gradient training. With it, every run converges and the late level reaches the paper's.

**Rejected after the fix.** Fewer minibatches per update (8, 16) gave *earlier* stops — the untrained network's greedy action is a fixed per-seed action that sometimes raises frequency. Raw Eq. (7) rewards (`reward_learning_scale=1`) destabilized training mid-run (late timeouts 7–13 %). t_u = 2 settled at 7.4–7.9 steps, below the paper's 8.2; t_u = 3 settles at 9.2–9.9.

**Gate calibration.** The paper shows one run, and the gates compare one run with it. To see how strict that is, each finished seed's deviation from its config's mean curve (inflated by √(N/(N−1))) was added to the digitized paper curve and gated with `fig4_gates`. Per-episode noise alone: 13/13 synthetic paper runs pass. Full seed-to-seed variance: 14/26 across seven configs and 5/13 on the final config. A replication whose mean matched the paper exactly would pass roughly 40–55 % of seeds; single-seed `gates.pass` is close to a coin flip even for a faithful run. Gates were not changed.

**Systematic shape gap → replay warm-up.** Without a warm-up the 13-seed mean declined gradually from ep 10 to 130 (0/13 seeds passed; the mean curve failed 3 gates), while the paper stays at 25 until ep ~60 and then drops within ~25 episodes. That flat-then-sharp shape is what a replay warm-up produces. `replay_warmup_transitions=1500` (~60 episodes × 25 steps; DQN "learning starts", paper-silent) reproduces it; 1,000 was too short.

**Result: `fig4_nguyen_config`.** 13 seeds of the final recipe:

| | Start | t10 / t50 / t90 | Late (150–500 / 350–500) | Ratio | Reward t50 / gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | 24.9 | 62 / 76 / 89 | 8.57 / 8.19 | 0.344 | 71 / 0.97 |
| 13-seed mean | 24.1 | 58 / 73 / 115 | 9.96 / 9.88 | 0.41 | 67 / 0.90 |

Seeds 0, 1 and 9 pass every gate; six more fail one gate, two fail two. The mean curve fails only `length_t50_timing` (t90 115 vs ≤ 114). The remaining systematic difference is the late plateau: ~1.7 steps above the paper, settling more slowly. The shipped figure is seed 0, the default seed.

Every Fig 4 artifact before this one — v130, v158, and the Fig 5–7 runs built on them — trained with frozen hidden layers and the spike-count tie bug.

## One network per episode (Oct 6 2026, rejected as official)

**Finding.** In carried mode the plant redraws the wiring permutations and per-neuron conductances on every 100 ms step for seeds without cached MATLAB draws ([plant.md](../../plant.md) § Carried segments). The 8 cached seeds instead keep one network. On those seeds, holding 80 Hz gives α–β ≈ 160 from step 10 on; on redrawing seeds the same hold gives ≈ 70. Redrawing keeps the network from synchronizing, so DBS suppresses α–β much more. `SNNConfig.plant_fixed_network=True` keeps one network per episode (`PythonPlant.integrate(fixed_network=True)`); non-carried runs and the default carried path are bit-identical to before.

## Authors' published code (Oct 12 2026)

**Source.** The follow-up (arXiv 2606.28600, Acknowledgments) links its code: [github.com/howyoubinh/CL-DBS-RL](https://github.com/howyoubinh/CL-DBS-RL) (MIT, one commit, "Initial public release", Jun 19 2026). It holds the CBGT simulator (`src/simulation/simulate_network_optimized.py`, a Python/Numba port of the Kumaravelu model), the Gymnasium environment (`src/environment/gym_pd.py`), the training loop (`scripts/train_rl_rockpool_16ch.py`) and the follow-up's checkpoints. arXiv still has only v1 of the follow-up; its Research Square copy (rs-10095848) carries the same supplement. The ISCAS paper has no arXiv version, and no thesis, poster, slides or dataset turned up. The group also presented a knowledge-distillation paper at IEEE NER 2025 (abstract only).

**Which parts are the Fig 4 paper's environment.** The base class `MousePDEnv` implements Eq. (7) term for term: $\tau - \delta E$ below θ, $-d - \delta E$ above it with $d = (\alpha\beta - \theta)^2$ unnormalized, and $\tau (t_r + 1) - \delta E$ on termination (`remaining_steps = max_steps - step_count + 1`). The trainer reaches it with `--legacy-reward`. It starts each episode at 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 and steps ±5 Hz / ±0.1 ms / ±5. The code is not exactly the ISCAS code, though: `step()` clips pulse width to 0.06–0.4 ms and amplitude to 0–250, so it cannot reach the ISCAS late 1.13 ms.

**What the code does that the papers don't say:**

| Topic | Published code | Ours (official recipe) |
|---|---|---|
| DBS into the STN | `+ Idbs[i]` added to every STN neuron's membrane equation: full current, no scaling, no spatial falloff (Kumaravelu, ρ = 1) | ρ = 3 distance-based recruitment (labelled deviation) |
| Waveform, solver | Monophasic rectangular pulses (`creatdbs`), forward Euler, dt 0.01 ms | Same |
| Pulse clock | Restarts at t = 0 in every 100 ms segment, so each segment opens with a pulse | Phase-continuous across segments |
| State carried between segments | Voltages, gates and calcium. Synaptic gating and the spike-delay buffers restart at zero every 100 ms | Voltages, gates, calcium and synapses |
| Wiring | `np.random.seed(seed)` at the start of every 100 ms call, so every step redraws the same wiring. The reset call draws initial voltages first, so the reset window's wiring differs from the steps' | A different draw every step ([plant.md](../../plant.md) § Carried segments) |
| Seed per episode | Drawn once on the first `reset()` and reused for every episode of the run (`if self.seed is None`) | New seed per episode |
| Reset window | `reset()` calls the simulator with its defaults, **100 Hz / 300 / 0.3 ms**, on a fresh network for 100 ms, then sets 40 Hz / 0.3 / 300 for the first action. Step 1 continues from the reset network | Fresh network at 40 Hz / 300 / 0.3 ms |
| Energy $E_t$ | `sqrt(mean(Idbs**2))` over the step (no N factor) | Same (`stimulated_neurons=1`) |
| Episode energy log | "Total Stimulus Energy" = sum of $E_t$ over the executed steps | Per-step mean × 25 |
| Spike log | "Avg Brain Spikes per Step": ones in the 100 × 80 binary matrix (1 ms bins, all 8 populations, STN and GPi included), summed over the episode ÷ length | Spike events per step without STN and GPi |
| Observation | 100 one-ms bins × 80 neurons | 10 bins × 80 neurons |
| Reward constants | τ = 3,000, θ = 150, δ = 0.01 (help text: "use 0.01 with --legacy-reward") | τ = 330, δ = 1 |
| t_u | Env default 5; the follow-up trainer turns it off (999) | 3 |
| Updates | One minibatch per env step once the buffer holds 128 transitions; soft target update every step | Every 128 transitions, 64 minibatches, 1,650 warm-up |
| ε | `0.05 + 0.85·exp(−steps / 2000)` | Linear 0.9 → 0.05 over 2,000 |

The follow-up's §2.1 says connectivity and initial conditions were randomized each training episode. In the code, the initial conditions are fresh each episode, but the seed (and with it the wiring and initial voltages) is the same in every episode of a run.

**What this bears on (as read before any training; outcomes in the next section).**

- **ρ = 3.** The group's own simulator drives every STN neuron with the full current. Under the rule that the authors' published implementation fills gaps, it gives no support for distance-based recruitment. The follow-up's energy-aware agent settles at 0.06 ms pulses (its §3.2), so on their plant too, wide pulses buy no suppression. That points to the ISCAS 1.13 ms being drift under a weak energy term (δ/τ = 0.01/3,000 in the code against our 1/330) rather than a plant effect. Batch D in the next section tested this: the code's constants leave late pulse width at 0.70 ms on ρ = 3, so they don't explain the 1.13 ms, and ρ = 3 stays. See [5.md](5.md) § Authors' published code.
- **Fig 7 step 0.** The 100 Hz reset window reproduces the paper's low start on the uniform plant. Adopted as the Fig 7 eval start (v29). See [7.md](7.md) § Adopted: the authors' reset window.
- **Paper-silent constants.** τ = 3,000, δ = 0.01, the ±5 amplitude step, 1 ms observation bins, one update per step and the exponential ε all come from the authors' code and differ from our conventions. The Fig 4 paper does fix the 128-transition cadence, so that stays. None of this is adopted until it has been trained and checked against the gates. Trained Oct 12 2026: § Training with the authors' code conventions.

## Training with the authors' code conventions (Oct 12 2026)

**Question.** Does following the authors' published code ([§ Authors' published code](#authors-published-code-oct-12-2026)) wherever the ISCAS paper is silent reproduce Figs 4–7 better than the official recipe, and does it let us drop the ρ = 3 plant deviation? Rule: what ISCAS states stays as written (25-step episodes, the 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 start, Eq. (7), spike-count argmax, a new eval seed per episode, its parameter ranges, which reach 1.13 ms); where it is silent, use the code.

**New `SNNConfig` fields** (all off by default; `fig4_nguyen_config` is unchanged):

| Field | Code value | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `reset_window_frequency_hz` | 100 | Runs the reset window (the 100 ms before the first action) at this frequency, then the initial 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 for the agent |
| `plant_pulse_restart` | true | Every 100 ms segment opens with a pulse at t = 0 (`creatdbs` per call) |
| `plant_restart_synapses` | true | Synaptic gating and spike-delay buffers restart at zero every segment; voltages, gates and calcium carry |
| `train_fixed_episode_seed` | true | Every training episode resets with the run's seed (the code draws one seed per env). Eval keeps a new seed per episode, as §IV states |
| `replay_truncation_terminal` | false | Bootstrap at the 25-step horizon; only a $t_u$ stop ends the return |

Already settable and set from the code: τ = 3,000, δ = 0.01, rewards unscaled for learning (`reward_learning_scale=1`), amplitude step ±5, 100 one-ms observation bins fed one row per SNN step (`sequence_steps=100`, `snn_input_mode=sequence`), ε = 0.05 + 0.85·exp(−steps / 2,000), $t_u$ = 5, and one minibatch per env step once 128 transitions are stored. The paper's "updating its weights after collecting every 128 state transitions" reads either way; the code's reading is one update per step after the first 128. The code's same-wiring-every-step plant is `plant_fixed_network=true`. The Fig 7 script takes `--set FIELD=VALUE` to evaluate a checkpoint with any of these.

Our runs at τ = 3,000 do overshoot the late level, though (ep 150–500 mean +32.5k on ρ = 1, paper −25.7k raw / −13.3k smoothed), while the official τ = 330 run sits at −27k. The reward gates are scale-free, so neither choice is gated on this.

## Network faithfulness audit (Oct 17 2026)

**What the audit found.** Three places where the DSQN departed from §III.B, which the earlier work had not caught:

- **Two hidden layers.** `DSQN` ran input → 128 → 128 → 9: an input Linear+LIF layer of 128 neurons, a hidden one of 128, and the output layer. §III.B states "a three-layer spiking neural network with a single hidden layer of 128 LIF neurons". This page had even ruled out borrowing the follow-up's 2 × 128 hidden layers (§ Authors' published DSQN implementation), but the code had them anyway.
- **Reset scaled by β.** `LIFLayer.step` stored $U - S\theta$ and leaked it on the next step, so the reset was $\beta\theta$, not Eq. (2)'s $S[t-1]\theta$. It also read Q from the membrane after the reset.
- **Static input.** The 10 × 80 spike matrix was flattened and held constant for 5 unrolled steps, and each layer passed spike counts (0–5) to the next. Eq. (4) gives the observation as binary spikes "across sequence steps" and the network operates "across multiple timesteps". The authors' code feeds one row of the matrix per SNN step.

`SNNConfig.hidden_layers` (1 = the paper) and `lif_reset` (`eq2` = Eq. (2) as written, Q read before the reset) are new. The old defaults stay so earlier checkpoints load. `snn_input_mode=sequence` already existed.

**Batches.** 8 seeds each (0–7), 500 episodes, ρ = 3. `scripts/probes/nguyen_seed_batch.py` drives them; outputs are under `artifacts/figures/papers/nguyen/4/candidates/audit{1,2,3}-<variant>-s<seed>/`. **What each batch settles.**

- **Network.** The paper's network trains at least as well as the old one and lands closer to the paper's late length and frequency. It is adopted because §III.B states it, not because it scores better.
- **Bin width.** 100 one-ms bins (the authors' code) trains less stably with the same learner. Late length doesn't hold (`length_late_plateau_hold` fails on 5 of 8 seeds) and late reward falls to −45.7k. 10 bins of 10 ms stay a convention. The paper gives no bin width (author question 12).
- **τ.** Under Eq. (7) the best possible episode earns $25\tau$ (every reading below θ, then the stop bonus), so the top of the paper's raw reward band pins τ. That band tops out at +48.4k again and again in episodes 346–369, which needs τ ≈ 1,940–2,000. τ = 2,000 reproduces it (+49.3k). But its late mean is +9k against the paper's −13.3k, and `reward_relative_gain` then fails on every seed: our late episodes stop sooner and cleaner than the paper's. τ = 1,000 reproduces the paper's late level (−11.3k to −12.6k) and passes the most Fig 4 seeds. The code's τ = 3,000 would put the ceiling at ~75k, and the old τ = 330 at ~8k; both are ruled out. τ = 1,000 is adopted; the conflict between the ceiling and the late mean goes to the authors (question 2).
- **$t_u$.** The authors' code defaults to 5, and the paper gives no value. $t_u$ = 5 is the only change that brings late frequency to the paper's (83 vs 81 Hz). Late length (9.8–9.9) stays inside the gate band (8.19 ± 1.9), and pulse width and energy rise toward the paper's (0.81 ms, 1,475). Adopted. The old $t_u$ = 3 under-shot the frequency on every seed.
- **ε.** The authors' exponential schedule puts the length $t_{90}$ at episode 127 against the paper's 89. The linear schedule (t90 100) stays. This is the first test of the exponential schedule on a learner whose hidden layers train; the Oct 5 rejection ran on frozen layers.
- **δ and amplitude step.** The code's δ = 0.01 and ±5 nA/cm² change nothing measurable. Adopted as the code's values.

**Adopted recipe** (`fig4_nguyen_config`, Oct 17 2026): one hidden layer, Eq. (2) reset, one row per SNN step (10 × 10 ms bins), $t_u$ = 5, τ = 1,000, δ = 0.01, amplitude ±5, with everything else unchanged (ρ = 3, 64 minibatches, 1,650 warm-up, linear ε). Seed 0 is shipped: it passes Fig 4 and widens the pulse to 1.06 ms late (paper 1.13).

**What no setting closes.** Across all 88 runs, a handful of gates fail on nearly every seed whatever the knob:
- Fig 5 `energy_overshoot`: the paper's energy peaks around episodes 150–350 and then falls 16 %; ours stays flat.
- Fig 6 late α–β level: finals ~97 against 109 ± 5.
- Fig 6 late α–β spread (CV ~0.28 against 0.16).
- Fig 6 late frequency, which only $t_u$ = 5 fixes.

These are the late operating point on our plant, the same gap § Retuning for distance-based STN recruitment and [5.md](5.md) § Dose-response trace to how DBS reaches the STN. Under the recalibrated gates, a run whose mean matched the paper exactly would pass all of Fig 6 on one seed only about half the time (49 %). Ours fails these rows systematically, not by chance.
