## Why α–β climbs after step ~14 (Oct 6 2026)

Paper mean (refined digitization, per step): 146 at step 0, peak 278 at step 2, ~170 by step 6, then 150–160 to step 25. v26 mean: 329 at step 0, falls monotonically to 96 at step 14, then climbs to 176 by step 25.

**The policy never holds.** In all 50 v26 episodes the greedy policy raises every parameter almost every step: frequency 40 → 79 Hz, amplitude 300 → 451 nA/cm², pulse width 0.30 → 1.16 ms. Training episodes end after ~9 steps (`t_u` sub-threshold steps), so it never sees states past ~step 10 and never learns to stop.

**The late climb is the high-charge STN block** ([5.md](5.md) § High-charge STN block). By step 15 charge per pulse is ~300 nA·ms/cm² and by step 25 ~520; on the open-loop map 450 × 1.1 at 60–80 Hz silences the STN and α–β returns to ~285. Frequency alone would keep α–β near 170.

**Step 0 is open.** Our reset step (DBS at 300 / 40 / 0.3 from the plant's initial conditions) reads 182–284 on probe seeds 0–2 and 329 on average over the 50 eval seeds. The paper's 146 → 278 rise over the first two steps looks like a start-up transient that our initial conditions do not produce; the paper does not describe its reset.

**Step-0 hypotheses ruled out (Oct 6 2026).**

- *Estimator.* The follow-up computes α–β with a multitaper point-process estimator on GPi spike times (NW = 3, K = 5, 7–35 Hz, 100 ms windows). Ours is the same Chronux estimator averaged over GPi neurons, which reproduces Fig 3's levels.
- *Plant start-up transient.* Holding the initial 300 / 40 / 0.3 setting, mean α–β over steps 0–6 is ~320 → 280 on 20 eval seeds and ~290 → 360 on the 8 seeds with cached MATLAB initial conditions. No step-0 dip like the paper's 146.
- *Network state carried across episodes* (DBS parameters reset, neurons continue). With the v26 policy, the eval mean flatlines at ~275 for every step, unlike the paper's shape; the follow-up also randomizes initial conditions per episode. Not adopted.

`paper_start_near_paper` (153 vs our ~330) and `paper_peak_step_timing` (paper peak at step 2) depend on that unexplained onset.

## What the late windows need (Oct 6 2026)

**Energy weight alone does not help.** Retrains at δ = 2, 3, 5 keep charge low (no STN block) but the policy still raises frequency every step (to 84–146 Hz) and α–β sinks to 30–110. v26's late mean passed only because the block pushed α–β back up. Six τ / δ pairs (τ 33–330, δ 1–50) and ε-greedy eval (ε 0.05, 0.1) give the same picture.

**The paper's late plateau looks like threshold tracking.** Scripted policies on the shipped plant (20 eval seeds):

| Policy | α–β from step ~5 |
|---|---|
| Raise frequency every step | falls to ~25 |
| Ramp to 80 Hz, hold | ~75 |
| Raise frequency when α–β > θ, lower it when below | ~150 (paper ≈ 150–160) |

Training episodes end at the first sub-threshold streak, so the agent almost never acts below θ and does not learn the "lower it" half.

**On one network per episode the late windows pass** (every run, [4.md](4.md) § One network per episode): 80 Hz holds α–β near 165, close to the paper's plateau. That plant fails Figs 4–6, so it stays an opt-in variant.

**Onset.** On both plants step 0 reads ~320–330 and α–β falls from there; the paper starts at ~146 and peaks at step 2. Also ruled out: the reference script's own GPi 7–35 Hz measure (`make_Spectrum`, grid spanning first to last spike, spectra averaged over cells) gives the same onset (328 vs our 320 at step 0), and GPi neurons fire within the first 3 ms. Neither paper says what step 0 measures, so `paper_start_near_paper`, `paper_peak_step_timing` and `paper_pearson_shape_ok` stay failing.

## More onset and plateau checks (Oct 7 2026)

**Stimulation off during the reset step.** If reset simulated its first 100 ms without DBS and the 40 / 0.3 / 300 setting began at step 1, step 0 would be a DBS-off reading. On the 50 eval seeds (seed 1 + 10,000 + episode), DBS off reads 297 ± 14 (95 % CI of the mean) at step 0; DBS on reads 329 ± 16. Neither is near the paper's 153. Ruled out.

**Evaluation that ends episodes early.** Recomputed from the shipped 50-episode eval by cutting each trajectory where $t_u$ sub-threshold steps end it (the greedy policy is deterministic, so the cut trajectories are what a terminating eval would record). With $t_u$ = 1–3 the median episode ends at step 5–9 and almost none run past step 13. Averaging only running episodes leaves nothing to average late; carrying each episode's last reading forward gives a late level of 106–114. Neither gives the paper's ~153 plateau. Ruled out.

**Spread band.** Our per-step standard deviation across the 50 episodes is 40–60 (up to 100 past step 16). The paper's shaded band has half-widths of 50–100. That matches ±1.96 SD of single episodes. A 95 % CI of the mean would need an SD of 180–360, so the band is most likely an episode spread, not a CI of the mean. No gate uses the band.

**Shape offset.** Our mean from step 1 (282, 215, 194, 173, 146) follows the paper's from step 2 (278, 259, 206, 190, 171), but falls faster. The paper adds two low readings first (153, 252). That is about 0.55 and 0.9 of the step-2 peak, a two-step ramp that neither paper explains. The follow-up states 100 ms analysis windows, which rules out a longer zero-padded window.

## Training past the $t_u$ stop (Oct 7 2026, probe only)

The paper ends a training episode after $t_u$ sub-threshold steps, so the agent rarely acts below θ. `terminate_on_subthreshold=False` (`SNNConfig`, off by default) keeps every training episode running to the 25-step horizon. It uses Eq. (7)'s non-terminated reward each step and only flags where the episode would have stopped. This deviates from a stated mechanism and is not adopted. Everything else is the Fig 4 recipe. ## How the paper might have plotted step 0 (Oct 7 2026, hypotheses only)

Every plant-side explanation above failed, so these test how the paper's figure could have been drawn from readings like ours. Both reuse existing 50-episode evals and transform only how readings are arranged for plotting; neither changes the controller or the plant. Neither paper describes either one, and the official Fig 7 is unchanged. An earlier version of the eval inserted *invented* step-0 values and was reverted (see the top of this file); these use only plant readings.

- **H1, stale step 0.** Step 0 is the last reading of the *previous* episode (a logging convention where the recorder holds the last value until the new episode's first reading arrives), and every reading shifts one step right. On this hypothesis the paper's 153 start and wide band (75–218) are the previous episode's late level, and its rise to 278 is our reset reading.
- **H2, running average from 0.** Each plotted point is an exponential running average $m_t = a x_t + (1-a) m_{t-1}$ with $m_{-1}=0$, $a = 0.5$.

| Eval | As shipped | H1 stale step 0 | H2 running average |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official Fig 4 (seed 1) | 4 fails, $r$ = 0.61, RMSE 53 | 0 fails, $r$ = 0.85, RMSE 37 | 0 fails, $r$ = 0.81, RMSE 35 |
| Trained past $t_u$, seed 4 | 3 fails, $r$ = 0.56, RMSE 44 | 0 fails, $r$ = 0.87, RMSE 22 | 0 fails, $r$ = 0.91, RMSE 21 |
| Trained past $t_u$, seed 1 | 3 fails, $r$ = 0.58, RMSE 45 | 0 fails, $r$ = 0.89, RMSE 23 | 0 fails, $r$ = 0.94, RMSE 23 |

$r$ is `pearson_mean_trace`; RMSE is against the digitized paper mean over steps 0–25. Gate fails count `fig7_eval_gates` gates (Fig 4 lineage aside). H1 peaks at step 1 (~330) where the paper peaks at step 2 (278); H2 peaks at step 1 but too low (~222). Neither is confirmed: both would mean the paper's figure does not show plant readings at step 0, which only the authors can settle. Not adopted.

## More plotting variants (Oct 8 2026, hypotheses only)

Twelve further arrangements of the same three evals, to see whether any also puts the peak at step 2. Same rules as above: plant readings only, nothing adopted, official Fig 7 unchanged. ## H3 with a true 200 ms window (Oct 8 2026, probe only)

H3's 2-reading mean stands in for α–β measured over 200 ms. To test the real thing, three 50-episode evals (same checkpoints and seeds as above, behaviour unchanged) logged every segment's GPi spike trains, and α–β was re-measured over each pair of consecutive segments (200 ms) with the same `alpha_beta_power`. Band power in this code grows with window length (a 200 ms window reads ~2× two 100 ms readings), so 200 ms values are halved to the 100 ms scale that Fig 3 and $\theta$ use. Plotted with the one-step lag and the previous episode's buffer kept across the reset:

## Reset-order slip with a carried network (Oct 8 2026, probe only)

A plant-side version of H1, using real readings only. Two changes to how an eval episode starts, alone and together (50 episodes, same seeds; probe script patches the adapter and plant, nothing in the repo changes):

- **Reset-order slip.** The reset's 100 ms integrate runs at the *previous* episode's last DBS setting; the 40 Hz / 300 / 0.3 ms setting takes over from step 1. Each episode still starts its control from the paper's initial parameters; only the first observation comes earlier. Episode 0 uses the initial setting.
- **Carried network.** The plant keeps its neuron state across the episode reset (the seed still changes per episode, so noise and draws differ).

## Episode-start convention and gate audit (Oct 12 2026)

**Question.** Slip + carry passes every gate on the official checkpoint. Is it a convention the papers leave open (adoptable with a label) or a departure from something they state?

**What the texts say.**

- Nguyen et al. §IV: "Each episode starts with initial DBS parameters: frequency of 40 Hz, pulse width of 0.3 ms, and amplitude of 300 nA/cm²."
- Nguyen et al. §IV: "We evaluate our trained model against 50 test episodes using a random seed for each episode to emulate patient-specific neural responses in PD."
- Nguyen et al. §III.B builds the environment on Gymnasium, whose `reset` returns the first observation of a new episode from a fresh initial state.
- Follow-up (arXiv 2606.28600) §2.1: "network connectivity and initial conditions were randomized for each training episode". Algorithm S1, lines 8–9: "Reset ℰ; obtain initial state $\mathbf{s}_1$", "Reset all LIF membrane potentials in $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\theta'}$". §3.1 starts "from an initial stimulation state (40 Hz frequency, 250 μA amplitude, and 0.3 ms pulse width)". No warm-up, burn-in or discarded transient is described; the only warm-up in the paper is a sparsity-loss schedule.
- The follow-up's Appendix F runs seven 500-step blocks back to back, and state carries between blocks ("a persistent resonant oscillation that takes time to naturally decay"). That is a disease-switching experiment within one run, not a set of episodes, and says nothing about Fig 7.

**Decision: not adopted.** Both parts of slip + carry go against stated text, so neither is a paper-silent convention:

1. **The slip** runs the episode's first 100 ms at the previous episode's final setting. §IV says each episode starts with the initial parameters.
2. **The carry** keeps the previous episode's neurons. §IV draws a new seed per episode to emulate a new patient's response. The follow-up randomizes initial conditions per episode. Gymnasium's reset starts a new episode from a fresh state. In our redrawing plant the seed already changes the wiring each step, so carrying only the membrane state would keep the previous "patient's" neurons under a new seed.

Adopting it would be a deviation from stated mechanisms. The plotting hypotheses H1 and H3 aren't adoptable either: they move or average readings when plotting, so step 0 would no longer be a reading from that episode. That is the kind of display change the replication rules exclude. The official eval keeps the fresh-network reset reading at the initial setting.

**Where the paper's step 0 seems to come from.** The paper's band at step 0 (75–218) matches its own late band (mean edges over steps 18–25: 87–217). Ours at step 0 spans 220–413 (2.5–97.5 % of episodes), against a late band of 61–220 and a last reading of 131 (61–211). The paper's step-1 band (149–367) looks like our step 0. So the paper's step 0 looks drawn from the treated, late-episode distribution of the previous episode. A stale logged value (H1, H3) and a carried network (slip + carry) both give that, and only the authors can say which (questions 1, 2 and 4 above).

**Why no checkpoint closes the onset gates.** Step 0 is recorded before the first action, so the policy has no effect on it: 321 ± 15 on the 50 eval seeds (onset map above). A peak at steps 1–3 would need the policy to push α–β above a fresh network's start, and the mean-trace $r$ is pulled down by that same step-0 excess. Retraining or evaluating other seeds can't change these three gates. This is structural, not a tuning gap.

**Gate audit.** Every gate was checked against `curves_fig7.json`, which is normalized from the refined WebPlotDigitizer project. Fixes, none of them looser:

- `eval_protocol_ok` checked `≥ 1 episode and ≥ 20 steps`, although its description said ≥ 50 episodes. It now requires the paper's 50 episodes, and requires every episode to cover the paper's 26 readings (the band is traced at steps 0–25). `plot.py` used to pad short episodes with their last value before gating; the gates now get the trajectories as recorded.
- `peak_step_timing` was a hard-coded `{1, 2, 3}`. It is now the paper's peak step (2.11, rounded) ± 1, which gives the same set from the digitization.
- `below_fig3_pd_median` now also requires the overall mean to sit below the paper's Fig 3 PD-On readout (~295), not just below our own Fig 3 median (295.2).
- The tracker descriptions now give the paper values and tolerances. The start was listed as ~160.4, but the gate has always used 153.2.
- The plot's shaded band is the 2.5–97.5 % range of episodes and is now labelled that way. It used to be called a "95% CI".

Tolerances (±25 % levels, ±30 % ratio, ±50 % drop, $r$ ≥ 0.80) are unchanged. v28 re-gates the shipped v27 eval: 11 of 14 pass, with the same three onset fails.

**Figs 3–6 on the same commit.** `--plot-only` re-gates from the cached Fig 3 samples and the shared Fig 4 series, written to scratch paths and not promoted. All four still pass. The only false rows are report-only: Fig 5 spike and energy levels, and Fig 6 amplitude and parameter stability.

**Fig 5 late energy.** No untried paper-silent knob is left. The stop bonus τ, minibatches per update, warm-up and ρ = 3.5 have all been run over 13 seeds and leave late pulse width at 0.71–0.79 ms ([5.md](5.md) § Dose-response and the late energy gap). Biphasic pulses and coarser integration steps don't make width matter on the uniform plant either (§ Pulse shape and integration step). What remains is a plant change (ρ ≈ 4, which fails Fig 4) and the energy-bookkeeping question for the authors. Both are documented in 5.md.

## Reset window in the authors' code (Oct 12 2026, probe only)

**Finding.** The group's published environment ([4.md](4.md) § Authors' published code) does not run the reset window at the initial setting. `MousePDEnv.reset()` calls the simulator with its default arguments, **100 Hz / 300 / 0.3 ms**, on a fresh network for 100 ms, and returns that window's spikes and α–β. Only then does it set 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 for the first action, and step 1 continues from the reset network. Episodes never carry over: each reset builds a fresh network. That is the paper's "Each episode starts with initial DBS parameters" for the agent's settings, with a reset reading taken under 100 Hz.

**Probe.** The step-0 reading of a fresh network on the 50 Fig 7 eval seeds, plant settings from the v27 checkpoint (a one-off variant of `scripts/probes/nguyen_fig7_onset_map.py` with the reset window at 100 Hz; not kept. The full eval is now `plot.py`'s default, below):

|---|---|---|

| ρ = 3 (official) | 197 ± 15 (range 114–312) | 321 ± 15 |
| Paper Fig 7 step 0 | 146–153 (gate 115–191) | |

(± is the 95 % CI of the mean.) On the uniform plant the code's reset lands on the paper's step 0. The network then leaves the 100 Hz state once the agent's ~40 Hz takes over, which fits the paper's rise to a peak at step 2 (153 → 252 → 278). On ρ = 3 the 0.3 ms reset pulses recruit less of the STN, and step 0 sits just above the gate.

## Adopted: the authors' reset window (Oct 12 2026)

**Decision.** The official Fig 7 eval runs each episode's reset window the way the authors' published environment does (github.com/howyoubinh/CL-DBS-RL, `MousePDEnv.reset()`): the fresh network's first 100 ms run at 100 Hz / 300 nA/cm² / 0.3 ms, and that window is the step-0 reading. Then the parameters are set to the paper's 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300 nA/cm² for the agent's first action. `plot.py` applies `reset_window_frequency_hz=100` on top of the checkpoint's config by default; `--initial-setting-reset` reproduces the earlier 40 Hz reset.

**Why it is faithful.** The ISCAS paper says each episode starts with the initial parameters and is silent on what the reset reading runs at. Its authors' published code fills that gap, under the rule that the group's own implementation is the faithful reading where the paper is silent. Nothing in it contradicts stated text: the agent still starts every episode at 40 Hz / 0.3 ms / 300, each episode is a fresh network with its own seed, and no state carries over.

**Eval only.** Training is unchanged: `fig4_nguyen_config` and the official checkpoint stay as they are. Training with the reset window (batch D, [4.md](4.md) § Training with the authors' code conventions) changed nothing beyond seed noise.

**Result (v29).** Same checkpoint, same 50 seeds as v28. Steps 0–6: 197, 211, 213, 195, 181, 178, 168 against the paper's 146, 252, 278, 259, 206, 190, 171. 13 of 14 gates pass: the peak moves to step 2 and the mean-trace $r$ rises from 0.71 to 0.88. Only `start_near_paper` fails, with step 0 at 197 ± 15 against the gate's 115–191. On ρ = 3 the 0.3 ms reset pulses recruit less of the STN than on the uniform plant (140 there), so this residual gap ties to the STN-coupling question (question 6 above) rather than to the episode start. The peak (213 vs 278) and the late window (128 vs 153) sit low but inside their gates.

**What it replaces.** v28 (40 Hz reset, 11 of 14) and the slip + carry best match (`eval_50ep_slipcarry_v2.png`), which needed two episode-start guesses against stated text. Both stay in the history above.

## Step 0 under the authors' code (Oct 13 2026)
